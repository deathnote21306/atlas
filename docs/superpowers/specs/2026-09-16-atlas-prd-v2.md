## 01 Purpose, Vision & Product Principles

ATLAS is one Africa-focused intelligence platform with two complementary commercial
motions: governments use it as an institutional intelligence and coordination layer;
private users access the same public intelligence layer through scalable subscriptions
for monitoring, market-entry, opportunity discovery and capital intelligence.

**Product mission:** Make African markets easier to understand, easier to navigate and
easier to invest and do business in.

**Product principles**

- One platform, not separate government and investor products: one codebase, one country
  model and one public intelligence layer, with packaging and permissions determined by
  entitlements.
- Recurring value comes from change, not static profiles: What Changed, alerts,
  watchlists and briefs must make ATLAS useful every week or month.
- Depth before breadth: a small number of genuinely useful Full Intelligence markets is
  better than 54 shallow profiles.
- Information governance is a product feature: every private-to-shared-to-external
  transition must be explicit, revocable and auditable.
- Neutrality is non-negotiable: ATLAS Public remains sourced and analytically neutral;
  client-provided content is clearly labeled.
- ATLAS informs decisions; it does not make them. No investment recommendation,
  underwriting, brokerage, negotiation or success-fee model.

## 02 Goals, Success Metrics & Non-Goals

**Primary product goals**

1. Make ATLAS the first place a professional opens to understand what is happening in an
   African market, who matters, what changed and where to look next.
2. Create a daily/weekly operating layer for government analysts and institutional teams,
   not a presentation-only tool used during roadshows.
3. Let companies monitor the countries where they operate, map local ecosystems and
   identify material changes without rebuilding research manually.
4. Enable governments to coordinate across ministries, agencies, sovereign vehicles and
   SOEs without pooling all confidential information into one undifferentiated repository.
5. Create a controlled bridge from internal government coordination to selected external
   investors, DFIs, banks, operators and business partners.

**North-star metric:** Number of institutions and professional teams for which ATLAS
replaces a recurring manual country-intelligence, monitoring or coordination workflow.

**Key success metrics**

| Area | Metrics |
|---|---|
| Government | Paid pilots; annual conversion; weekly active analysts; What Changed views; Monthly Brief use; shared dossiers; approval cycle time; Approved External items. |
| Private | Explore registrations; watchlist activation; free-to-Core conversion; Core/Pro paid organizations; retention; countries followed; saved searches; opportunity screens. |
| Trust | Source freshness; stale-data rate; correction turnaround; provenance completeness; permission incidents; audit completeness; security findings. |
| Economics | Government ACV; Core/Pro ARR; Enterprise ACV; data cost / ARR; gross margin; expansion ARR / NRR. |

**Non-goals**

- No investment recommendation, buy/sell/invest signal or automated credit decision.
- No financial structuring, brokerage, transaction execution or deal success fees.
- No automatic disclosure of private government or corporate data.
- No generic social feed for collaboration; collaboration is organized around dossiers,
  projects, tasks, approvals and ownership.
- No opaque country score replacing professional judgment.

## 04 Users, Personas & Roles

| Persona | Primary job | Key ATLAS workflow |
|---|---|---|
| Government Analyst / Adviser | Maintain current country intelligence and prepare recurring analysis. | What Changed, macro/sector/risk monitoring, briefs, source review. |
| Government Manager / Director | Coordinate teams and prepare decisions. | Dashboards, shared dossiers, task/approval status, peer benchmark, monthly brief. |
| Institution Admin | Manage users, institutional content and sharing rights. | Permissions, ownership, workspace membership, internal sharing. |
| National / Authorized Approver | Control external disclosure. | Approve selected fields/documents for named external recipients; revoke access. |
| Regional Operator / Corporate | Monitor countries where the company operates or is expanding. | Watchlists, regulation, sectors, companies, infrastructure, What Changed. |
| Investor / Fund / Bank / DFI | Find where to look next before deeper diligence. | Country comparison, opportunity screening, capital activity, government-approved content. |
| Private Enterprise Admin | Integrate team research and govern private workflows. | Private organization layer, team spaces, SSO/API, internal notes. |
| External Recipient | Consume approved project information only. | Investor data room, approved documents, updates, Q&A where enabled. |

**Product role model**

- Analyst: create and edit within allowed workspace; cannot approve external disclosure.
- Manager: review, comment, assign tasks and share within allowed internal scopes.
- Institution Admin: manage institution members, ownership, internal permissions and
  policy settings.
- National / Authorized Approver: final authority for Government Shared to Approved
  External transitions.
- ATLAS Admin: operational least-privilege access only; no business discretion over
  client information.
- External Recipient: access limited to named data rooms / approved items; no visibility
  into Institution Private or Government Shared content.

## 05 Platform Architecture & Navigation

ATLAS should feel like one platform whose navigation adapts to account entitlements. The
public intelligence layer is shared; government and enterprise workflows appear only when
the user has the relevant rights.

**Global navigation**

| Area | Pages / modules |
|---|---|
| Core navigation | Africa Home; Countries; Compare; Briefs; Watchlist; Search; Account. |
| Pro navigation | Core + Opportunities; Capital Activity; Saved Searches; advanced alerts/comparison. |
| Government navigation | Home; Country Intelligence; Projects & Pipeline; Investors & Markets; Collaboration; Documents; Analytics & Reports; Data Governance / Admin. |
| Private Enterprise navigation | Country Intelligence; Opportunities; Capital & Investors; Team Workspace; Documents; Analytics & Reports; Admin / Integrations. |

**Country information architecture**

- **Overview:** executive summary, What Changed, key metrics, country signals, market
  positioning, top sectors, risk snapshot, Generate Monthly Brief.
- **Macro / Economy:** GDP, growth, inflation, debt, currency, reserves, fiscal/current-account
  context where available, trends and watchpoints.
- **Politics & Institutions:** institutional architecture, political calendar, government
  priorities, recent developments and policy implications.
- **Sectors:** priority sectors, structural drivers, constraints, major actors, momentum
  and recent developments.
- **Companies:** local champions, SOEs, multinationals and strategic private companies.
- **Finance:** banks, DFIs, sovereign/public finance institutions, PE/infra funds,
  institutional investors, capital-market infrastructure and financing activity.
- **Infrastructure:** roads, ports, rail, airports, energy, telecom/digital, priorities and
  operators.
- **Regulation:** tax, FDI/ownership, FX/repatriation, import-export procedures,
  incentives and watchpoints.
- **Risks:** political, sovereign/credit, FX, legal/regulatory, ESG; sovereign ratings;
  peer risk comparison.
- **Compare / Sources / Reports:** peer view, methodology/provenance and generated outputs.

## 06 Living Country Intelligence Requirements

The recurring experience is Understand → Track → Position → Compare → Discover → Brief.
Every country page should expose freshness and source provenance rather than behave like a
static annual profile.

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **CI-001** | Country Overview | Displays executive summary, key metrics, top sectors, risk snapshot, What Changed and Market Positioning for every Full Intelligence country. |
| **CI-002** | Eight intelligence pillars | Macro, politics, sectors, companies, finance, infrastructure, regulation and risks exist as structured modules. |
| **CI-003** | Sources & freshness | Every material claim / metric exposes source, period/data-as-of and latest retrieval or update date. |
| **CI-004** | Missing data behavior | Missing or unverified information displays N/A / unavailable; AI must not fill gaps with invented facts. |
| **CI-005** | Coverage status | Country clearly labeled Full Intelligence, Standard Intelligence, Benchmark Data or Coming Soon. |
| **CI-006** | Cross-linking | Entities link across modules where useful: company → sector → country; project → sponsor → sector; DFI → activity. |
| **CI-007** | Responsive hierarchy | Critical signals and changes appear above long descriptive research; dense detail remains drillable rather than overwhelming the overview. |

### 06B What Changed, News & Positioning

**What Changed**

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **WC-001** | Visible high on each Full Intelligence country page and on watchlist / home feeds. |
| **WC-002** | Each item includes category, effective date, short summary, source, materiality (Low / Moderate / High) and previous/new state when applicable. |
| **WC-003** | Supported categories include macro/fiscal, politics/institutions, regulation, sovereign rating, FX/monetary, infrastructure, energy/mining, major companies, DFI/investor activity and major financing/project developments. |
| **WC-004** | Personalized "Since Your Last Visit" can use the user's previous visit; otherwise use a clear recent reference window. |
| **WC-005** | If no material change exists, show "No material change identified" rather than inventing filler content. |

**News & Events**

- Curated and decision-relevant; not a generic 200-article news feed.
- Each item: date, category, source, concise summary and optional materiality.
- Important items may feed What Changed and Monthly Briefs; editorial duplication should
  be avoided.

**Market Positioning & Peer Benchmark**

- Market Positioning shows structural strengths, key constraints, capital readiness,
  priority sector positioning and recent direction of change.
- Prefer transparent labels such as Strong / Moderate / Weak and Improving / Stable /
  Deteriorating with rationale over opaque 0-100 scores.
- Peer Benchmark supports a relevant default peer set and user-selected comparison across
  growth, inflation, debt, FX stability, ratings/risk, regulation, infrastructure,
  financial depth and sector diversification where data exists.
- Comparison should show where the country outperforms, where it lags and what changed
  versus peers.

### 06A Monthly ATLAS Brief

The brief is a core retention feature: a short institutional country output generated from
information already present in ATLAS. It must not invent new facts.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Header | ATLAS Monthly Country Brief - [Country] - [Month Year] + data-as-of date. |
| 1. Executive Summary | Concise synthesis of the country state and material developments. |
| 2. What Changed | Most material deltas during the period. |
| 3. Macro Update | Key macro movements supported by ATLAS data. |
| 4. Political / Regulatory | Material institutional and regulatory changes. |
| 5. Sector Developments | Priority-sector developments relevant to business and capital. |
| 6. Risk Update | Material risk changes with source/rationale. |
| 7. Sovereign / FX | Included when relevant. |
| 8. Next Watchpoints | Evidence-based issues to monitor; not investment recommendations. |
| 9. Sources | Sources, retrieval dates and data-as-of statement. |

- Target length: 1-2 pages in the MVP logic.
- Users can preview before export; generated brief remains tied to underlying source items.
- If a section lacks sufficient evidence, omit it or mark data unavailable rather than
  generating generic text.

## 09 Collaboration, Permissions & Data Governance

**Trust rule:** PRIVATE NEVER BECOMES EXTERNAL BY DEFAULT. Every transition from
Institution Private → Government Shared → Approved External must be explicit,
permissioned, revocable and auditable.

**Information layers**

| Layer | Who can see it | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ATLAS Public | Users with relevant access | Official/public data, sourced ATLAS analysis, public news, company/sector profiles, public project information. |
| Institution Private | Authorized users inside one client institution | Internal notes, models, documents, early project context, institution-owned analysis. |
| Government / Organization Shared | Selected institutions / teams | Shared dossiers, common project facts, approved working docs, tasks, comments and coordinated updates. |
| Approved External | Named eligible external recipients | Government-approved opportunity facts, sector briefs, project fields and selected documents. |

**Required permission controls**

- Per-item visibility label and owner institution.
- Named-recipient external access where applicable.
- Role-based permissions by user and organization.
- Approval history: approver, version, timestamp, recipient scope and business rationale.
- Revocation without deleting the internal record.
- Version history, disclosure logs, exports/download logs and owner attribution.
- No bulk cross-institution access merely because institutions share the same national
  environment.

### 09A Government Collaboration Workspace

Collaboration is organized around shared dossiers, project records, approvals and
institutional ownership, not an undifferentiated national data pool.

| Requirement | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Shared dossiers | Created only when multiple institutions need to coordinate around a topic/project. Membership is explicit. |
| Comments & activity | Threaded comments and chronological activity tied to a dossier or project, not a generic social feed. |
| Tasks | Assign owner, institution, due date and status. Show My Tasks and overdue items. |
| Documents | Every document has owner, version, visibility, updated-at and approval state. |
| Approval queue | Central view of items awaiting Manager / Institution Admin / National Approver action. |
| Audit | All changes to visibility, approvals and external access are logged. |

**Design principle:** ATLAS improves inter-agency efficiency without forcing every
institution into one undifferentiated data pool. Shared workspaces are created only where
coordination is needed.

## 10 Project Workspace & External Data Room

The project workspace is the bridge between government coordination and approved external
visibility. Internal institutions coordinate first; selected information is then approved
for external recipients.

**Project workspace record**

| Area | Required fields / features |
|---|---|
| Identity | Project name, country, sector, sponsor/lead institution, implementation agency, status/stage, last updated. |
| Summary | Project description, strategic rationale, economic/operational context, sources and owner. |
| Capital | Estimated capital requirement, disclosed funding need, financing status where appropriate. |
| Milestones | Timeline, milestone owner, status, target date and completion date. |
| Impact | Jobs/beneficiaries/output metrics where sourced and approved; methodology/source visible. |
| Risks | Risk categories, rationale, owner and visibility layer. |
| Contributors | Participating institutions and designated contacts. |
| Documents | Versioned files with visibility, owner, approval status and external-share eligibility. |
| Workflow | Internal preparation → Government coordination → Inter-institution review → Government approval → External sharing. |
| Activity | Comments, updates, approvals, document actions and system events. |

**External data room**

- A filtered view of the same project record, not a manually duplicated copy.
- External recipients see only fields/documents explicitly approved for them.
- Support named recipients such as funds, DFIs, banks, operators, corporates or advisers.
- Show sharing status, recipients, last approval/update and next steps.
- Support revocation and re-approval when a new version replaces an older externally
  shared document.
- Internal-only risk assessments, commercial notes or procurement files remain
  inaccessible unless explicitly approved.

## 11 Business Ecosystem Intelligence

ATLAS must answer: "Who matters in this market?" This makes the product useful to
operators and corporates, not only financial investors.

| Object | ATLAS shows | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Companies | Local champions, SOEs, multinationals, strategic private companies. | Competitor, partner and market-structure intelligence. |
| Public institutions | Ministries, regulators, agencies, investment authorities, sovereign vehicles. | Understand institutional architecture and relevant counterparts. |
| Financial ecosystem | Banks, DFIs, PE/infra funds, institutional investors, capital markets. | Know who provides financing and who is active. |
| Infrastructure operators | Ports, utilities, rail, telecom and logistics operators. | Understand operating constraints and partner landscape. |
| Economic / industrial zones | Zones, clusters, hubs and incentives. | Support market-entry and expansion decisions. |
| Projects / programmes | Public projects, concessions, PPPs and strategic programmes. | Identify where commercial and investment demand is emerging. |

**Entity page requirements**

- Company/institution name, type, sector, country, concise description, source,
  ownership/listing where public, and relevant related entities.
- Search/filter by company type, sector and country.
- Strategic Actor labels may be used, but must have a transparent rationale and avoid
  implying endorsement.
- No unsourced personal contact data or sensitive personal information.

## 12 Opportunity & Capital Intelligence

**Opportunity Intelligence - Pro / Enterprise**

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **OP-001** | Surface sourced public and government-approved projects, concessions, PPPs, privatizations, strategic programmes and selected corporate expansion opportunities. |
| **OP-002** | Filters: country, sector, stage, sponsor, capital requirement, ticket size, currency, DFI involvement and disclosed attributes. |
| **OP-003** | Opportunity card: title, country, sector, sponsor, stage, source, date, key facts, information status and approval label. |
| **OP-004** | Government-approved opportunities must be visually distinguished from ATLAS Public information. |
| **OP-005** | No synthetic investment recommendation or opaque "ATLAS opportunity score". |
| **OP-006** | Saved searches and alerts available for Pro/Enterprise users. |

**Capital Activity Intelligence**

- Track public/verified deployments by DFIs, funds, banks, strategic investors and public
  institutions.
- Allow filters by country, sector, investor type and period where data supports it.
- Show source and transaction/activity date; do not infer undisclosed ticket sizes or terms.
- Capital Activity provides context around where financing is moving; it is not a
  recommendation engine.

**Scenario Intelligence - later Pro / Enterprise**

- Contextual directional scenarios for FX depreciation/convertibility stress, commodity
  movements, refinancing environment, sovereign rating/outlook changes and selected
  macro/regulatory shocks.
- Every scenario must show assumptions, methodology and uncertainty. No black-box
  investment verdict.

## 13 Market Interest Intelligence & Express Interest

A later network feature unlocked by broad private adoption. It turns platform engagement
into privacy-safe market-sensing intelligence for governments.

| Signal | Example output | Privacy requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Country attention | Number of organizations viewing a country over a period. | Aggregate only; suppress small cohorts. |
| Sector momentum | Relative change in attention to sectors. | No individual organization identity by default. |
| Geographic source | Top origin countries/regions for attention. | Only display when statistically safe. |
| Opportunity views | Most-viewed government-owned/approved opportunities. | Only for content government owns or approved. |
| Search interest | Rising searches for sectors/themes. | Aggregated and anonymized. |

**Express Interest - opt-in only**

- Pro or Enterprise user can voluntarily express interest in a country, sector or
  opportunity.
- User chooses whether the signal is shared with the relevant government.
- Organization identity remains hidden unless the organization explicitly opts in to
  disclosure.
- User can revoke future visibility of the interest signal subject to audit/history rules.
- ATLAS does not broker, negotiate, recommend or charge a transaction success fee.

## 14 Search, Watchlists, Alerts & Briefs

**Universal search**

| ID | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **SR-001** | Search countries, sectors, companies/institutions, opportunities/projects, briefs and documents the user is allowed to access. |
| **SR-002** | Permission filtering happens before results are returned; private/shared/external content cannot leak through search snippets. |

**Watchlists**

- Users can follow countries; Pro/Enterprise can also save opportunity searches and richer
  thematic filters.
- Watchlist dashboard prioritizes What Changed, high-materiality alerts, new briefs and
  notable sector/regulatory developments.
- Entitlement limits follow product tier; alerts should not expose content outside
  purchased/authorized markets.

**Alerts**

- Alert triggers: material What Changed item, new sovereign rating/outlook, material
  regulatory update, new approved opportunity, saved-search match or important project
  update.
- Notification frequency should support immediate/high-materiality plus digest modes;
  exact channel mix is an implementation decision.
- Every alert links to the underlying sourced item, not only AI-generated summary text.

## 15 Data Model, Sources, Freshness & AI

**Source hierarchy**

1. Official / primary sources: ministries, central banks, statistics offices, regulators,
   investment agencies and public operators.
2. Multilateral sources: IMF, World Bank, AfDB and other trusted institutions.
3. Market, rating, company, operator and regulator sources where licensed or legally usable.
4. Secondary sources only where necessary, with clear provenance and confidence.

**Required metadata on material facts**

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Source | Source name + source URL/reference where legally possible. |
| Period / effective date | When the data/fact applies. |
| Retrieved / updated at | When ATLAS last retrieved or validated it. |
| Owner | ATLAS Public, client institution, government shared owner, or approved external owner. |
| Visibility | Public / Institution Private / Government Shared / Approved External. |
| Status | Verified / pending review / stale / unavailable where applicable. |
| Version | Version identifier for governed documents and externally shared content. |

**AI rules**

- AI summarizes and connects grounded ATLAS information; it does not invent missing facts.
- Generated briefs cite or link to underlying ATLAS source items and preserve data-as-of dates.
- When evidence is insufficient, output N/A / insufficient evidence rather than filler.
- Scenario outputs disclose assumptions, confidence and methodology.
- No automated investment recommendation, underwriting or credit approval.

**Freshness operations**

- Country fields and content objects require expected refresh cadence or review policy.
- Stale content should be visibly flagged in the product and in internal data-ops queues.
- Correction workflow must preserve previous version / audit history for governed content.
- Country expansion is gated by the ability to maintain source freshness, not by
  superficial profile count.

## 16 Security & Non-Functional Requirements

| Stage | Minimum control set |
|---|---|
| Public Intelligence | Strong authentication, HTTPS, RBAC, secure secrets, backups, logging and vulnerability testing. |
| Enterprise Private Layer | Strict tenant isolation, encryption, granular roles, audit logs, retention controls, external pentest and incident plan. |
| Approved External Sharing | Named-recipient access, revocation, disclosure logs, versioning, explicit owner approval and export/download controls. |

**Core security requirements**

- Authorization is server-side and data-layer enforced; the UI alone is never the security
  boundary.
- Tenant and institution identifiers are required on private/shared objects.
- Audit events are immutable or protected against ordinary user modification.
- Exports/downloads of governed content are logged.
- External data-room links must not provide public anonymous access unless explicitly
  designed for public content.
- Revoking access should immediately prevent future access while preserving audit history.

## 18 Acceptance Criteria & QA

**R0 launch acceptance - public intelligence**

| Area | Launch criteria |
|---|---|
| Country coverage | At least one genuinely deep Full Intelligence market (initially Gabon) + selected peer/benchmark coverage with explicit labels. |
| Country modules | All required pillars render with sourced data or explicit N/A behavior; no invented gaps. |
| What Changed | Material updates display category/date/source/materiality; no-change state works. |
| Peer benchmark | User can compare home market with configured peers; source periods are visible. |
| Monthly Brief | 1-2 page brief can be generated from current ATLAS data with data-as-of and sources. |
| Search / watchlist | User can search allowed entities and follow countries; home/watchlist reflects material changes. |
| Data trust | Material metrics/claims expose provenance and freshness; stale items are identifiable. |
| Security | Authentication, basic RBAC, logging, backups and secret management are operational. |
| Pilot readiness | Government Intelligence can be deployed public-data-first without requiring confidential government uploads. |

**Enterprise collaboration acceptance - R2**

- Institution Private content is not visible to another institution unless explicitly shared.
- Government Shared workspace membership is explicit and testable.
- External access requires an approval event and named scope; no default external visibility.
- Revocation removes external access without deleting the internal content/audit record.
- Every externally shared version is traceable to approver, timestamp, owner and recipient scope.
- Search and exports respect permissions.

**Critical QA scenarios**

- User A in Institution 1 cannot infer Institution 2 private content through search, URL
  guessing, API or export.
- An outdated externally shared document can be replaced without silently exposing a newer
  internal-only version.
- A generated brief never cites content the user cannot access.
- What Changed does not treat routine low-value news as material by default.
- A country with incomplete data degrades gracefully rather than generating synthetic
  certainty.

**Scope discipline:** Every new feature must support at least one of: Understand → Track →
Discover → Coordinate → Share. Features that do not strengthen a recurring workflow or the
network should be deferred.

## 20 Out of Scope & Open Decisions

- Deal Analysis / DCF / IRR recommendation engine.
- Automated credit scoring or investment approval.
- Transaction negotiation, brokerage, deal execution or success fees.
- Full 54-country deep coverage.
- Heavy Private Enterprise integrations before paid demand exists.
- Confidential government production data before Enterprise security controls and external
  pentest readiness.
- Uncontrolled public marketplace of projects.
- Opaque proprietary country score presented as investment truth.
